HP Envy 17-cr0xxx repair guide: screen, hinges, battery and cooling

2. Tools and supplies

Tools

  • Precision Phillips screwdrivers, #00 and #0 (the battery kit includes basic ones)
  • Plastic spudgers, opening picks and a guitar-pick-style pry tool
  • Small suction cup (helps lift the screen)
  • Hair dryer, to soften screen adhesive
  • Anti-static wrist strap
  • Magnetic screw mat, or an egg carton and masking tape for labeling screws
  • Plastic tweezers
  • Phone camera, to photograph every step
  • Safety glasses and thin gloves, since you're handling cracked glass

Supplies

  • Isopropyl alcohol, 90% or stronger
  • Lint-free cloths or coffee filters, and cotton swabs
  • Compressed air (hold fans still while you blow them out)
  • Soft brush, such as a clean paintbrush or toothbrush
  • Thermal paste
  • Thin stretch-release adhesive strips
  • Two-part epoxy (such as JB Weld) and optionally M2 or M2.5 brass threaded inserts, for rebuilding a broken hinge mount
  • Kapton or electrical tape, to re-secure any cable tape you lift
  • A soft towel to protect the lid and the new screen

3. Before you start

Get your BitLocker recovery key first

Windows 11 Pro often encrypts the drive automatically. Disconnecting the battery or changing hardware can make the laptop ask for a BitLocker recovery key at the next startup, and without it you can't get into Windows. Sign in at aka.ms/myrecoverykey with your Microsoft account, find the key for this laptop, and write it down or print it before you open anything.

  • Save your current battery report (you already have one) to compare with the new battery afterward.
  • Run down the battery to about 25%, then shut down. A partly drained lithium battery is safer to handle and disconnect.
  • Shut down fully: hold Shift while clicking Shut down, so the laptop doesn't just hibernate.
  • Battery delayed? You can start now. Skip Phase 4, reconnect the old battery in Phase 9, and do the short Phase 10 when the new battery arrives. The old battery (77% health) is fine in the meantime.
  • Download HP's service manual for the HP ENVY 17.3 inch Laptop PC 17-cr0000 from HP's support site (document N16796-001), and keep it open next to you. Its Section 5 covers removal: bottom cover (page 33), battery (page 34), display panel (page 45), display assembly (page 53) and keyboard/top cover (page 58).
  • Set up a large, clean, well-lit table, with somewhere to rest the open lid flat.

Back up your files first

The repair doesn't touch the drive, but a dropped screwdriver or a static shock can. A few minutes of backup means nothing is lost if something goes wrong. Pick one:

  • External drive, by hand (simplest): plug in a USB drive with enough free space, open File Explorer, and copy your Desktop, Documents, Pictures, Videos, Music and Downloads folders onto it. Open a few copied files from the USB drive to make sure they work.
  • File History (automatic): Control Panel → File History → select the USB drive → Turn on, then Run now. It keeps copies of your user folders and can restore older versions later.
  • OneDrive: if you already use it, open the OneDrive icon by the clock and check it says "Up to date." Only folders set to back up are included (Settings → Sync and backup → Manage back up).
  • Browser and passwords: if you're signed in to your browser, bookmarks and passwords are already synced. If not, export bookmarks (and passwords, to a file you then keep safe or delete after the repair).
  • Licences and anything else unique: game saves, app settings, product keys and 2FA backup codes.

Waiting for the lid? Using the laptop with an external monitor

It's generally safe to run the base on its own with the display disconnected, as long as it's set up carefully:

  • Keep the cooling intact: heatsink screwed down with fresh paste, fan cables connected, and the vents unblocked. Don't run it with the heatsink off.
  • Cover or protect the open board: no screws, metal tools or loose plastic on or under it. Put the bottom cover back on if you can (a few screws are enough), or set the base on a clean, dry, non-metal surface.
  • Protect the loose cables: tape any unplugged display, touch, webcam and antenna connectors to a non-metal spot so they can't touch the board.
  • Power: reconnect the battery, or at least use the charger. The power button is on the keyboard, so you can switch it on normally.
  • Display: plug in HDMI before powering on. Windows should show the picture on the external monitor, since it detects no built-in screen. If it powers on but the monitor stays black, press Windows + P and then Enter a couple of times to cycle display modes.
  • BitLocker: because the battery was disconnected, Windows may ask for the recovery key at startup. That screen may only appear on the external monitor once Windows starts the display, so have the key ready (aka.ms/myrecoverykey, from another device). If the screen stays black for more than a few minutes, it may be waiting at this prompt where you can't see it; hold the power button to shut down and try again, or wait for the lid.
  • Wi-Fi: the Wi-Fi antennas live in the lid, so with the lid off the signal is weak or gone. What worked here: keep the antennas connected to the card and stick them back onto the old lid in their original spots, lid laid nearby. Otherwise lay them flat, apart from each other and away from metal (taped to cardboard). If they're unplugged, use USB phone tethering or a USB Wi-Fi or Ethernet adapter. A Wi-Fi card running briefly without antennas won't be harmed.
  • Keep it still: the loose hinges and open case are easy to knock. Don't move it while it's running.

6. After the repair

  • If Windows asks for a BitLocker key, enter the one you saved.
  • Check that touch is still turned off in Device Manager under Human Interface Devices. A new screen can sometimes re-enable it.
  • Run HP's built-in diagnostics: power on while tapping F2 and run the fan and battery tests.
  • Check the new battery: after a full charge, run powercfg /batteryreport. Full Charge Capacity should be close to 55,000 mWh. If it reads below about 50,000 mWh, return it within Amazon's window.
  • Check the screen for dead pixels or uneven brightness while you're still within its 3-month warranty.
  • Use it gently: open the lid from the middle, not one corner, and keep it open on a desk where possible. The hinge design that failed hasn't changed.

7. Lessons learned

What this repair taught, for this laptop and the next one.

LessonWhat happened and what to do next time
Diagnose before orderingThe hinges were fine; the plastic mount in the lid had broken. Opening it first would have pointed straight to the lid and saved the hinge set (keep it as spares). Next time, remove the display and look at the mount before buying parts.
It's a known weak pointBroken hinge mounts are reported across many HP Envy 17 generations, including the 17-cr0000. Open the lid from the middle of the top edge, never by one corner; check the hinge screws when the bottom is off; and act early if the lid starts to flex or creak at a corner.
The Wi-Fi antennas live in the lidThey're thin gold foil stuck inside the lid, with cables running through the hinge to the Wi-Fi card. Taking the lid off weakens Wi-Fi a lot. Peeling them off can tear them: warm the adhesive and lift slowly with a plastic tool.
Labels and QR codes identify partsScanning the antenna labels gave the exact codes (DC33002NM00 / NM10). HP doesn't sell these antennas separately; they come with the genuine lid "with antenna" (N13573-001).
Genuine can be the better dealAftermarket lids were about $75 but usually lack antennas. The genuine lid (about $140) fixes the mount and replaces the torn antenna in one part.
The laptop can run while you waitWith cooling intact and nothing loose on the board, the base runs fine on an external HDMI monitor. Have the BitLocker key ready after any battery disconnect.
Antenna connectors are delicateThey're tiny press-fit snaps under a black cover on the Wi-Fi card. Lift straight up, press straight down until it clicks, and reconnect as few times as possible.
Photograph everythingCable routing, connector positions and labels. Your photos are the only record of how this exact laptop goes back together. Add the best ones to this guide (see the images folder in the README).

9. If something goes wrong

ProblemLikely cause and fix
No picture, but the laptop powers onDisplay cable not fully seated, at the board or at the screen. Disconnect the battery and reseat both ends.
Flickering, lines or a dim screenPartly seated or pinched display cable. Reseat it and check the routing near the hinge.
No Wi-Fi, or very weak signalAntenna cables loose or swapped, or an antenna not stuck in its spot in the lid. Check both are clicked onto the Wi-Fi card (MAIN and AUX as in your photo) and the black cover is back on. With the lid off, the antennas must sit flat, apart, away from metal; sticking them to the old lid in their original spots worked best.
An antenna connector won't stay on or doesn't clickIt's off-center or tilted. Lift it straight off, re-center and press straight down with a flat tool. If it still won't hold, the connector or socket may be bent; look closely under magnification. A torn antenna or damaged connector can be replaced with the spare from the old lid (DC33002NM00 / NM10). See SparkFun close-up photos.
Fan doesn't spin, or the laptop overheatsFan cable unplugged, or a cable or fragment touching the blades. Check both fans.
Won't power on at allBattery not connected, or a connector loose. Try with the charger plugged in, and reseat the battery connector.
Asks for a BitLocker recovery keyNormal after a battery disconnect. Enter the key from aka.ms/myrecoverykey.
Hinge feels loose againMount not fully rebuilt or screws not biting. Reopen and reinforce the mount, or replace the keyboard deck or lid.
Keyboard stops responding after repeated arrow-key pressesA known issue on this model, present before the repair, with no permanent fix from HP. Workarounds: keep the repeat rate and delay at their slowest (Control Panel → Keyboard), remap the arrows with Microsoft PowerToys Keyboard Manager, use the number-pad arrows with Num Lock off, or scroll with the touchpad. A restart restores the keyboard.
Left USB plug won't go in cleanlyCover not seated, or the port itself is bent. Check the port's alignment with the cover off.
Screen sits unevenly in the lidAdhesive too thick, or old adhesive left behind. Release the strips with their pull tabs, clean and refit.

1. Why Linux, and what to expect

Windows 10 no longer receives security updates, and this laptop has no TPM security chip, so it can't officially move to Windows 11. A lightweight Linux distribution such as Linux Mint Xfce gives it a modern, supported, privacy-friendly system, and it doubles as a low-risk way to try Linux before deciding anything about the Envy.

Good at

  • Web browsing, email, documents, notes
  • YouTube and streaming. Linux browsers often cap streaming at 720p, but this screen is only 768 pixels tall, so you lose almost nothing
  • Playing local video and music, and driving a TV over HDMI
  • A workbench or 3D-printer-side laptop: checking prints, browsing models, light slicing
  • Fitting in a small backpack, thanks to the short 21:9 shape

Not suited for

  • Heavy multitasking with many browser tabs, especially before the memory upgrade
  • Slicing very complex 3D models quickly, since it's a 2-core processor from 2012
  • Modern games
  • Anything that needs Windows-only software

3. The case: coating, replacement parts and 3D printing

Replacement case parts: rare and overpriced

The U845W's unusual shape means its case parts were never common. The new old-stock bottom and palmrest listings found are priced in the thousands, and the cheaper AliExpress listings for U840W/U845W case parts are no longer available. Watch eBay for a used U845W sold "for parts" at a low price; that's the realistic source of a cleaner case. Otherwise, refinishing your existing case is the way to go, and it works well.

Removing the sticky soft-touch coating

The "rubber" is a soft-touch paint that breaks down with age into a gummy layer. Taping over it doesn't hold, because adhesive won't bond to the goo and it oozes out at the edges. Strip it instead:

  1. Work on parts, not the whole laptop, where you can. Clean the bottom cover once it's off the laptop (Phase 2), so no liquid gets near the electronics.
  2. Use 70–90% isopropyl alcohol on a microfiber cloth or cotton pads. Rub small areas; the coating dissolves into sticky residue. Change pads often, and expect several passes.
  3. For stubborn areas, use a plastic scraper, or a barely damp melamine sponge (Magic Eraser) with light pressure.
  4. Never use acetone or harsh solvents. They can melt or cloud the plastic underneath.
  5. Finish with a final wipe of clean alcohol, and let it dry fully.
  6. Optional finish: a vinyl laptop skin or wrap sheet cut to fit (a cutting template is a good small project), or leave the bare plastic.

Can you 3D print a new case?

Short answer: not the whole case, but plenty of useful parts

  • Too big for the bed. The P2S prints up to 256 × 256 × 256 mm, and the U845W is about 369 mm wide, so any full shell would have to be printed in pieces and joined, which leaves seams in a structural part.
  • No model to start from. Toshiba never released the case design, so you'd need to measure and model every screw post, clip, port opening and vent by hand. That's a large reverse-engineering project.
  • Heat and strength. The bottom gets warm near the processor. PLA softens at fairly low temperatures; PETG or ASA would be needed, and thin printed walls flex more than the original injection-molded plastic.

A great project, but not the practical fix. Section 8 lists printable parts that are worth doing.

4. The clicking left hinge

A click on opening can come from three things. Check them in this order, because the third matters most:

  1. A loose hinge screw. The easiest fix: tighten it gently with the bottom off (Phase 2). Some owners of other laptops found that tightening the hinge fixed the noise; one ThinkPad owner found "the right hinge was so tight that it needed almost a 1/4 counter-clockwise turn to get rid of the noise," so adjust both sides evenly.
  2. A dry hinge. Laptop hinges are friction hinges with grease inside, and when it dries out they can creak or click. Lubrication can help (below).
  3. A cracking mount. This is the important one, given what happened to your Envy. With the bottom off, look closely at the plastic around the left hinge's screws. Any hairline crack, white stress marks, loose screw posts or plastic crumbs mean the mount is starting to fail. Reinforce it with epoxy now, while it's an easy fix.

How to lubricate it safely

  • Use a thick, non-conductive grease, such as silicone grease or dielectric grease, applied with a toothpick to the pivot where the hinge turns. One repair writer notes that laptop hinges "require a special thick lubricant" and that spray or oil alone often doesn't last.
  • A very small drop of light machine oil, like sewing-machine oil applied with a syringe, is an alternative. One Pinebook owner said that cured their clicking hinge.
  • Use very little. Too much lowers the friction that holds the screen up; as one HP forum reply put it, over-lubricated hinges may have "insufficient friction to keep it stable."
  • Never use WD-40 or any spray near the board. Sprays drift onto electronics, and WD-40 isn't a lasting lubricant for this.
  • Work the lid open and closed slowly 10–20 times to spread the grease, then wipe away any excess.

Replacement hinges for this model weren't available in any listing I found, so lubrication, tightening and reinforcing the mount are the realistic options.

7. Installing Linux Mint and the new battery

Why not clone the old drive onto the SSD?

Cloning copies Windows and everything else as-is onto the new drive. It isn't the right fit here: Windows 10 no longer gets security updates, so you'd be moving an unsupported system onto new hardware, and Linux is going on the SSD anyway. The Windows partition is also about 455GB, larger than the 256GB SSD, so a straight clone wouldn't fit without shrinking it first. Keeping the old drive untouched gives you the same safety net with less risk. If you ever do want Windows back, the old drive (or the optional Rescuezilla image) can restore it.

ProblemLikely cause and fix
Nothing happens; no new drive appearsReseat the connector and try another USB port directly on the laptop. Then open Disk Management (right-click Start → Disk Management). If the drive is listed but has no letter, right-click its partition → Change Drive Letter and Paths → Add. If it's listed as "Not initialized," don't initialize it; that can erase it. Unplug it and copy from it another way.

5. Reusing the drives afterward

Only once you've copied and checked everything, you can erase a drive and reuse it. Plug it in, right-click it in File Explorer → Format, choose exFAT (works with Windows, Mac and Linux) or NTFS (Windows only), and click Start. This erases everything on it.
